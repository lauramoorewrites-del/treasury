- Ana Vieyra, ["On a recent controversy regarding clarity in philosophical writing,"](https://epochemagazine.org/88/on-a-recent-controversy-regarding-clarity-in-philosophical-writing/) *Epoché Magazine* (2026). Accuses Muñoz of "logocide" — killing philosophy by demanding clarity — and defends obscurity as a feature of semantically rich thought.
- Daniel Muñoz, ["Is Daniel Muñoz an agent of the death of philosophy itself?"](https://bigifftrue.substack.com/p/is-daniel-munoz-an-agent-of-the-death) *Big iff True* (2026). Muñoz's response. He distinguishes between obscurity caused by difficult *ideas* and obscurity caused by poor *expression*, and argues that truly understanding something means being able to explain it at multiple levels.

> When I got older and read philosophers like Wittgenstein and Russell and Fanon – or the essays of Virginia Woolf – it occurred to me that there are few thoughts so complex that they can’t be expressed in clear, accessible prose. It’s a discipline. You keep working at the sentence until it does exactly what you intended, no more and no less. I didn’t use to write like that, and I still don’t write with the clarity I’d like.

— Zadie Smith, “Some Questions from *El Cultural*,” in [*Dead and Alive*](https://bookshop.org/a/113685/9780593834688) (Penguin Press, 2025).

> The only purpose of philosophical style is to make our meaning clear to the mind of another.

— Christian Wolff, *Preliminary Discourse on Philosophy in General* (1728), §141, trans. Richard J. Blackwell (Bobbs-Merrill, 1963); via [Virginia Karnstein](https://substack.com/@virginiawkarnstein/note/c-280752610), *Overlong Memories* (2026).
