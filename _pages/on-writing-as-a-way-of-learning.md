> I get my education in public by writing about things that, until I actually do write about them, I don’t already really know all that much about. I read up, I think through, I write out, and, the hope is, at the end I am a bit smarter about the subject under study.

— Joseph Epstein, introduction to *Life Sentences: Literary Essays* (W. W. Norton, 1997); quoted in Robert Minto, [“The disappointing memoir of a great essayist,”](https://registerofaliens.substack.com/p/the-disappointing-memoir-of-a-great) *Register of Aliens* (2026).

> I think I must read Ibsen & Shakespeare & Racine. And I will write something about them; for that is the best spur, my mind being what it is; then I read with fury & exactness; otherwise I slip & skip: I am a lazy reader. But no: I am surprised & a little disquieted by the remorseless severity of my mind: that it never stops reading & writing; make me write on Geraldine Jewsbury, on Hardy, on Women – is too professional, too little any longer a dream amateur.

— Virginia Woolf, diary entry, 28 November 1928, collected in *The Diary of Virginia Woolf*, vol. III.
