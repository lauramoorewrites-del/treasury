> …listening to Biggie’s rhythms or Bach’s triads, moving through Fred Moten’s arguments or Shakespeare’s scenes or occupying Octavia Butler’s alternative realities – whenever I am confronted with the art-productions of other people, I am always made acutely aware of one fact: I don’t see that way. Art is one of the ways we reveal the peculiarities of consciousness – for me it’s the clearest way.

— Zadie Smith, “Conscience and Consciousness: A Craft Talk for the People and the Person,” in [*Dead and Alive*](https://bookshop.org/a/113685/9780593834688) (Penguin Press, 2025).

> I feel I’m not the kind of writer who sounds quite the same from book to book – although maybe this is only a delusion of mine. And when I put on my reader’s hat, I have to acknowledge that there is always a family resemblance between the disparate things writers make. A shared sensibility.

— Zadie Smith, “Conscience and Consciousness: A Craft Talk for the People and the Person,” in *Dead and Alive* (Penguin Press, 2025).

> And spend a little more time in that capacious mind that I never got to know very well in life but which is preserved for all eternity in words, thank God.

— Zadie Smith, “What Lodged in Her Mind: Remembering Hilary Mantel,” in *Dead and Alive* (Penguin Press, 2025).
