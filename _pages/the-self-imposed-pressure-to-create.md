I'm feeling burnt out at the moment. I just started with vigor an essay about Iceland's Bell but quickly tired out from overwhelm over how much work is needed to do it right. I've been working on it for just 4 hours, which is not long!

I'm coming up with far more essay ideas than I could possibly have time for, which is part of the overwhelm. Writing is my hobby; it is an obligation only I place on myself. So any urgency is self-imposed, and there's no reason why my work can't stretch to as long as it needs to, on a calendar scale—however many days my effort is extended across, that should be fine. And yet I feel time pressure. Why is that? I think it's because new ideas keep coming and building pressure, even as the completion pace slows down, the ideation pace doesn't. That's probably most of it. But then some I think is a desire to be publishing more frequently than I am. A desire for a higher pace of output than I have. Though I don't know where that's coming from, since, again, I have an entirely different profession and job as a lawyer, and writing is ostensibly a hobby. The thing is, I'd like more of these pieces to become real than I have time for. It doesn't feel like a neutral fact, whether I get around to an essay. There is an urge to say these things, in a form that gives them justice, and of course to develop them the way they deserve—and I don't fully know what I am going to say about an idea until I devote a significant time to working on it. In the meantime, these ideas are mere potential, and not getting to them feels like wasted potential. Again, this pressure is entirely internal. No one is clamoring for more from me. The ideas want to be given life. I suppose that's a necessary quality to their appearing in the first place. If they didn't want to live so badly, why would they come to me at all?
