I’ve always been interested in the people behind the works I love (books, films, houses) or behind achievements I find impressive and unaccountable. My essay [“The Birth of the Author”](https://libertiesjournal.com/online-articles/the-birth-of-the-author/) was born of this magnetic draw toward people, and my frustration that Barthes would sever that connection between person and work and that Ferrante would view an interest like mine as contemptible.

I’ve just discovered the [house tour](https://homeworthy.com/watch/inside-a-storied-artist-residence-nestled-within-the-historic-national-arts-club/) of portrait artist Michael Shane Neal. He occupies a rare studio in the National Arts Club space on Gramercy Park, which incidentally I walked past just last week and wondered at. I saw a young artist seeming to move in to the building, so they must provide space to artists old and new. I wish I had gone in—they have exhibitions open to the public.

Watching Michael Shane Neal’s tour of his studio and living quarters, I’m utterly smitten. Foremost with his person (those exquisite fashions! that Mandy Patinkin-esque coif!) and his studio of rich woods, white walls, plaids and tartan soft furnishings. He pointed out several pieces around the space, some from the Edwardian period—and I realized, that’s the overall vibe. An Edwardian gentleman’s space of exquisite taste.

I love an artist’s studio. I would envelope myself in that aesthetic if I could. (Other favorites include Frida Kahlo’s, which I’ve visited in person, and Tove Jansson’s.) My little lofted condo in my early 30s had tall windows at one end of a double-height living room and I gloried in that space.

Now I’m perusing Michael Shane Neal’s portraits and recalling how much I love portraiture. (I spy a likeness of [Amor Towles](https://www.michaelshaneneal.com/portfolio?itemId=74smr21ra3batinaaa8kpbk815i7x1), whose wonderful *A Gentleman in Moscow* I recently read.) He’s done a lot of politicians (including US presidents) and corporate-types, who I assume are quite the “gets” and/or pay well. Not my favorite. But I love this portrait of his mentor, the artist [Everett Raymond Kinstler](https://www.michaelshaneneal.com/portfolio?itemId=8t8n9e7oxz6mnbchm9d3giax8vbjx9).

I’ve gone through obsessive periods with paintings by Robert Henri, Sargent, Manet, Joaquín Sorolla, Anders Zorn. I used to visit the National Portrait Gallery frequently, when I lived in walking distance, all but eschewing the nearby National Gallery of Art (to be fair, it was a few blocks farther along, and I’m a fervid energy conservationist).

There must be a connection between my interest in the people behind interesting achievements, my long love of biographies, and my preference for portrait art.

Michael Shane Neal: “I’m training as an artist but I found that painting portraits is my passion.” I wonder what that is made of, that passion. I’d love to interview him to find out.
