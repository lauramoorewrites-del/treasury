> I do not know that in the ordinary affairs of life philosophy is of much more use than to enable us to make a virtue of necessity. By showing us the advantages of a step which we are forced to take, but would not of our own free will, it consoles us a little for its unpleasantness. It helps us to do with equanimity what we would rather not do.

— W. Somerset Maugham, entry for 1894, [*A Writer’s Notebook*](https://bookshop.org/a/113685/9780307473196) (Heinemann, 1949).

> I don’t suppose anyone’s life is ruled by his philosophy; his philosophy is an expression of his desires, instincts and weaknesses.

— W. Somerset Maugham, entry for 1896, [*A Writer’s Notebook*](https://bookshop.org/a/113685/9780307473196) (Heinemann, 1949).
