> Mr. Higginson,
> 15 April, 1862
> Are you too deeply occupied to say if my Verse is alive?
> The Mind is so near itself—it cannot see, distinctly—and I have none to ask—
> Should you think it breathed—and had you the leisure to tell me, I should feel quick gratitude—
> If I make the mistake—that you dared to tell me—would give me sincerer honor—toward you—
> I enclose my name—asking you, if you please—Sir—to tell me what is true?
> That you will not betray me—it is needless to ask—since Honor is it's own pawn—

— Emily Dickinson, Letter to Thomas Wentworth Higginson, 15 April 1862, as published in Cynthia Griffin Wolff, [*Emily Dickinson*](https://bookshop.org/search?q=9780201168099) (Knopf, 1986).
