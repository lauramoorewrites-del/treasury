> This [late-career writing by Sartre] fruitfully bolsters my sense that how we live our life is itself a work of creative expression, a text to be read by those who come after (with all the attendant complexities inherent in compiling texts and interpreting them). I hadn’t made a connection with Sartre’s work until reading your Introduction, and I’m certain I’ll be making something of it moving forward.

— Laura Cecile Moore, private correspondence (2026).

> Like Taylor Swift, widespread and confident misunderstandings of Bourdieu make him useful for mapping social scenes and tracing their influences on one another. Just like there are different fan and hater groups with entirely opposed ideas about what makes Swift a hero or a villain (or gay or straight!), there are several very different groups of academic Bourdieu fans and critics with sharply incompatible opinions about just who he was or what he argued. Ask a regular person about Swift and you can quickly learn a lot about their social position; ask a social theorist about Bourdieu and you’ll quickly discover much about their background and alliances.

— John Encaustum, [“Bourdieu as a Touchstone,”](https://blackthornhedge.substack.com/p/bourdieu-as-a-touchstone) *Blackthorn Hedge* (2025).

> And perhaps the truth about ourselves is always for others to decide anyway. … We are the main characters in the novels of our lives, yes, but it is always others who read us.

— Zadie Smith, “On Writing *The Fraud*,” in [*Dead and Alive*](https://bookshop.org/a/113685/9780593834688) (Penguin Press, 2025).

> A recent film, *The Hours*, presents Woolf in a way surely her contemporaries would have marvelled at. She is the very image of a sensitive suffering lady novelist. Where is the malicious spiteful woman she in fact was? And dirty-mouthed, too, though with an upper-class accent. Posterity, it seems, has to soften and make respectable, smooth and polish, unable to see that the rough, the raw, the discordant, may be the source and nurse of creativity.

— Doris Lessing, foreword to Virginia Woolf, *Carlyle’s House and Other Sketches*, ed. David Bradshaw (Hesperus Press, 2003); printed as [“Sketches from Bohemia,”](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2003/jun/14/classics.virginiawoolf) *The Guardian* (2003).
