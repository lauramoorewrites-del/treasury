> In skillful hands, the novel can give us greater insight into history than history itself does. One could, for example, put together a list of novels that would tell more about the history and psychological condition of the United States than a general history of the subject.

— Joseph Epstein, *The Novel, Who Needs It?* Via [Miller's Book Review](https://www.millersbookreview.com/p/sigrid-undset-kristin-lavransdatter).

> But I love fiction. I love the way it lies to tell the truth.

— [Zadie Smith](/treasury/topics/zadie-smith/), *Dead and Alive* (Penguin Press, 2025).

> Art is the nearest thing to life; it is a mode of amplifying experience and extending our contact with our fellow-men beyond the bounds of our personal lot.

— George Eliot, ["The Natural History of German Life,"](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/28289/28289-h/28289-h.htm) *Westminster Review* (1856).

> ‘A good novel tells us the truth about its hero – but a bad novel tells us the truth about its author.’ So said G. K. Chesterton. I can’t tell you the truth about the author of *The Fraud*: I don’t think I really know it.

— Zadie Smith, “On Writing *The Fraud*,” in [*Dead and Alive*](https://bookshop.org/a/113685/9780593834688) (Penguin Press, 2025); the Chesterton line is from *Heretics* (1905).

> What Woolf did for literature was to experiment all her life, trying to make her novels nets to catch what she saw as a subtler truth about life.

— Doris Lessing, foreword to Virginia Woolf, *Carlyle’s House and Other Sketches*, ed. David Bradshaw (Hesperus Press, 2003); printed as [“Sketches from Bohemia,”](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2003/jun/14/classics.virginiawoolf) *The Guardian* (2003).

> Literature does not bring back the people we have loved. Literary immortality has nothing to do with the real life and real death of real people. We create our fictions, we feed on these fictions, and it is the imagination that matters. The literary result can be excellent within the fiction – our imagination did a great job – and yet, in reality, especially in the eyes of its writer, it can turn out to be a failure. With some exaggeration – but of course exaggerations are useful for understanding ourselves – you could say that fiction works all the better when reality doesn’t upset the narrative with its lack of logic and incoherence.

— Domenico Starnone, interviewed by Zadie Loft, [“Domenico Starnone on Old Age, Ambition and the Literary Game,”](https://thelondonmagazine.org/interview-domenico-starnone-on-old-age-ambition-and-the-literary-game/) *The London Magazine* (2026).
