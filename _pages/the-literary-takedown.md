> A takedown that's any good will be hard to quibble with too much because it will ground judgment in argument, and give too powerful sense of the inadequacies of method, technique, and worldview. You shouldn't be giving the reader the opportunity to say, "I just don't agree."

— Leo Robson, interviewed by Leonard Benardo, ["Closer to Rude Than Snide,"](https://www.theideasletter.org/essay/closer-to-rude-than-snide/) *The Ideas Letter*.

## Examples

- Renata Adler, ["The Perils of Pauline,"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1980/08/14/the-perils-of-pauline/) *The New York Review of Books* (1980). On Pauline Kael.
- Roland Barthes, *Criticism and Truth* (1966; English trans. University of Minnesota Press, 1987). A takedown of Raymond Picard's attempted takedown of Barthes.
- Merve Emre, "Two Paths for the Personal Essay," *Boston Review*. On the essay's descent into confession and therapeutic disclosure.
- Martin Filler, "Philip Johnson at 75: The Power and the Paradox," *Architecture Minnesota* (1981). His first piece on Johnson.
- Tibor Fischer, ["Someone Needs to Have a Word with Amis,"](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/comment/personal-view/3594613/Someone-needs-to-have-a-word-with-Amis.html) *The Daily Telegraph* (2003). On Martin Amis's *Yellow Dog*.
- Michael Hofmann, ["Vermicular Dither,"](https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v32/n02/michael-hofmann/vermicular-dither) *London Review of Books* (2010). On Stefan Zweig. Contains the line: "Stefan Zweig just tastes fake. He's the Pepsi of Austrian writing."
- Robert Hughes, ["The Artist as Entrepreneur,"](https://newrepublic.com/article/105864/the-artist-entrepreneur) *The New Republic* (1987). On Julian Schnabel's memoir. Collected in *Nothing If Not Critical* (1990).
- Gary Indiana, ["Always Leave Them Wanting Less,"](https://harpers.org/archive/2020/06/always-leave-them-wanting-less-andy-warhol/) *Harper's* (2020). On Blake Gopnik's Warhol biography.
- John Banville, ["A Day in the Life,"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2005/06/23/a-day-in-the-life/) *The New York Review of Books* (2005). On Ian McEwan's *Saturday*.
- Lauren Oyler, "With and Against," *The Baffler*. On Roxane Gay's *Bad Feminist*.
- Lauren Oyler, "The Socialism Network," *Bookforum*. On Sally Rooney's novels of millennial intimacy.
- Lauren Oyler, ["Ha Ha! Ha Ha!"](https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v41/n19/lauren-oyler/ha-ha-ha-ha) *London Review of Books* (2019). On Jia Tolentino's *Trick Mirror*.
- Tim Parks, ["Gods and Monsters,"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1999/05/06/gods-monsters/) *The New York Review of Books* (1999). On the "Rushdie aesthetic."
- Dale Peck, *Hatchet Jobs: Writings on Contemporary Fiction* (The New Press, 2004). A collected volume of takedowns.
- Leo Robson, ["From the Fact-Checking Department,"](https://www.newstatesman.com/culture/2011/11/richard-bradford-amis-martin) *New Statesman* (2011). On Richard Bradford's *Martin Amis: The Biography*. Runner-up for the inaugural Hatchet Job of the Year.
- Leo Robson, ["He Lost It at the Movies,"](https://www.theideasletter.org/essay/he-lost-it-at-the-movies/) *The Ideas Letter* (2026). On A. S. Hamrah.
- John Updike, "Jake and Lolly Opt Out," *The New Yorker* (1979). On Kingsley Amis's *Jake's Thing*. Collected in *Hugging the Shore* (1983).
- James Wood, ["Human, All Too Inhuman,"](https://newrepublic.com/article/61361/human-inhuman) *The New Republic* (2000). On Zadie Smith, Thomas Pynchon, Don DeLillo, and "hysterical realism."
- James Wood, ["The Slightest Sardine,"](https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v26/n10/james-wood/the-slightest-sardine) *London Review of Books* (2004). On Randall Stevenson's academic history of post-war British literature.
