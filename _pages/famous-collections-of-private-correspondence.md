- *The Paston Letters* (1422–1509). The correspondence of the Paston family of Norfolk, spanning three generations: property disputes, marriage arrangements, lawsuits, household management, the texture of daily life in fifteenth-century England. Virginia Woolf opens *The Common Reader* with them, pairing the Pastons against Chaucer as a contrast between artless record and literary art. Both preserve authentic medieval speech, but the letters are raw where Chaucer is transmuting. Edited by James Gairdner; the complete library edition appeared in six volumes in 1904.

- Lord Chesterfield, [*Letters to His Son*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3361/3361-h/3361-h.htm) (1746–1771). An eighteenth-century English nobleman's correspondence to his son on manners, education, social conduct, and personal development — guidance on the fine art of becoming a man of the world and a gentleman.
